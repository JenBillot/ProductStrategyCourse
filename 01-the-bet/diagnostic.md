# Three-Axis Vulnerability Diagnostic

**Contextual Moat — 3/5**
Workflow depth × switching cost. Would users leave in a weekend if a competitor showed up?
*Score rationale*: The tool is already embedded in users’ day-to-day workflow and has been designed specifically around their information needs. Initial comparison with a competitor index also suggests that moving to an alternative would not necessarily deliver the same search experience. However, switching costs are not extremely high, as alternative AI/search products could potentially be introduced.
Named attacker: Competitor index 

**Data Advantage — 4/5**
Proprietary signal that compounds with usage. What do you see that OpenAI doesn't?
Score rationale: Our tool has access to proprietary, domain-specific content that general-purpose AI products do not. However, the compounding advantage is currently less clear. Usage only creates a stronger data advantage if feedback, evaluation results and user behaviour are systematically captured and used to improve the product over time.
Named attacker: N/A

**Platform Exposure — 3/5**
Encroachment risk × pivot speed. If Apple/Google/OpenAI ships your hero feature native, then what?
Score rationale: Exposure is relatively high because semantic search, grounded answer generation and conversational AI are increasingly becoming standard platform capabilities. The mitigating factor is pivot speed: tools such as Lovable make it easier to rapidly prototype, test and adapt the experience rather than relying on the underlying AI capability itself as the differentiator.
Named attacker: Salesforce / Agentforce, Glean, OpenAI

**Top Vulnerability**
The core capability of searching content and generating grounded answers is becoming commoditised, so the product cannot rely on the AI capability itself as its long-term differentiator.

**Confidence Level**
Medium – there is evidence around workflow fit and search performance, but the data-compounding advantage and longer-term platform threat still need to be validated.
